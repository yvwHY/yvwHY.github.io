Solo Choir

A wearable instrument that turns one singer into a choir — diatonic SATB harmony, re-synthesised in your own voice, in real time, on a laptop CPU.

▶ hero loop — the rig + the live harmony  ·  /media/sc-hero.mp4

The body is the interface for harmony. The research question: does sending harmony into and from the body change how a singer learns and performs it — compared with a screen or a speaker?

01 — the instrument

One body becomes a whole chord — at the same moment you sing.

A personally-trained neural voice-conversion model re-synthesises your live voice and adds diatonically-correct harmony voices. The human voice is monophonic — one note at a time; Solo Choir lets a single body sound a simultaneous SATB chord. That simultaneity is the literal extension of the body — a choir of one.

Two embodied modes of one idea:

Learn in-body — open-ear bone conduction places an independent harmony target into your head while you still hear your own real voice, so you train the interval by feel. Perform from-body — the synthetic choir radiates from a small cup at your mouth and blends with your live voice, reading as one person.

02 — the studio

See, shape, and hear harmony — then sing it in your own voice.

Hum a melody; the system transcribes it, auto-arranges voice-led SATB, and draws each voice as a draggable pitch-contour line. Reshape pitch, onset and duration freely; a light synth previews instantly, then the engine renders the edited score into a choir of your own voice — per-voice stems you can mix.

The old fixed parallel harmony becomes a special case of an editable form — nothing lost, generalised. The hero shifts from low latency to compositional control over harmony.

03 — the making

A hand-built rig: a wire armature wrapped in paper fibre.

The wearable is built by hand — a bent-wire skeleton wrapped in stiffened paper fibre, in the lineage of body-extension sculpture rather than a clean 3D print. The mouth cup stays rigid and light so a surface exciter can radiate the choir; the throat mic and bone-conduction transducer are mechanically isolated so the rig never feeds back on itself.

Test A — feedback / self-stacking: passed by ear. Worn and sung through, the bone-conduction-out + throat-mic-in loop survives with no ring and no gradual self-stacking. Test B confirmed a surface exciter can't usefully radiate through tissue — so perform-mode commits to the cup as a spatial source at the mouth. Honest engineering: full simultaneous SATB is offline-rendered; live runs two to three voices on CPU.

04 — research & contribution

from demo to research

The contribution is the integration: live, per-voice neural SATB harmony, in the singer's own voice, on CPU, with an editable score — delivered through the body.

Each ingredient has prior art — real-time diatonic harmonisers, one-singer-to-his-own-voices instruments, neural voice conversion, wearable vocal controllers — but no single system combines them, and none delivers an independent harmony target into the head to learn a part. That embodied-delivery claim is framed as a hypothesis to be tested, not a settled result.

  • evaluationbone conduction vs headphones vs speaker — which lets a singer hold a harmony part fastest and most in-tune (pitch-error telemetry; pilot)
  • baselinenaive code pitch-shift vs neural re-synthesis — A/B, no human subjects
  • positioningvocoder / talkbox lineage · Collier–Bloomberg harmoniser · Cococo · Holly+ · body-as-interface sound art
  • honestylive = 2–3 voices; full SATB = offline render. Liveness, not low latency, is the point.
