A vocal instrument that turns one singer into a choir. Sing, and it answers with soprano, alto, tenor and bass, generated live by neural voice models on a laptop CPU. A tabletop instrument with two wearable prototypes, a bone-conduction headband and an exciter collar, for feeling the harmony in the body.

About

art → design → tech

I work where the body meets sound and machine — building wearable instruments and real-time systems that let a person extend, feel, and learn through their own body.

A five-year route from fashion pattern-cutting through generative-AI media to computational arts — with one through-line that keeps surfacing: turning one voice into a choir. My thesis project, Solo Choir, makes that literal: a tabletop instrument that answers one singer with a four-part choir in real time, shown with two wearable prototypes that let the harmony be felt on the body.

Research interests: the body as an interface, embodied sound, wearable and tabletop instruments, and real-time voice AI.
